## 2. Read & Understand

### A New Phone or a Weekend Away?

Imagine you have enough money for one special purchase. You could buy a new phone, a good watch, or a piece of furniture. Or you could spend the same amount on a concert, a short trip, or a meal with people you care about. Which choice gives better value?

A possession is something you can keep. This can be useful because you may use it every day for years. A good bicycle, laptop, or comfortable chair can improve daily life. Some possessions also have emotional value because they were gifts or remind us of important people.

Experiences are different. A holiday or concert ends, but the memory can remain. Psychological research has often found that spending money on experiences can be strongly connected with happiness. One reason may be that experiences are often shared with other people and become part of the stories we tell about our lives.

However, this does not mean buying things is always a bad idea. If you need a reliable laptop for work, spending money on it may reduce stress every day. A trip that causes debt may not feel like money well spent after you return.

There is also another problem: people are different. One person may look back on a weekend in another city for years. Another may receive greater satisfaction from something useful that lasts a long time.

Perhaps the better question is not “things or experiences?” but “What will add the most value to my life after the excitement of paying for it is gone?”

### In Short

A **possession** can be useful for years, while an experience may create a lasting **memory**. Research suggests experiences can bring strong **satisfaction**, especially when shared, but the best choice still depends on whether something feels like **money well spent** and what you will **look back on** positively later.

## 4. Conversation Toolkit

### Explaining What Matters Most

**For me, the most important thing is...**

> For me, the most important thing is making memories.

**For example...**

> For example, I still talk about a trip I took five years ago.

**I would rather... because...**

> I would rather travel because I value new experiences.

**It is worth it if...**

> It is worth it if I will use it for a long time.

## 6. Discussion

### 1. Your Money

What purchase has given you the best value in the last year?

### 2. Your Memories

What experience would you happily pay to repeat?

### 3. A Difficult Choice

Would you choose a new phone or a weekend trip if both cost the same?

### 4. Gifts

Are experiences better gifts than physical objects?

### 5. Age

Do people value possessions and experiences differently as they get older?

### 6. The Big Question

Is happiness something money can buy if we spend it in the right way?

## 7. Speaking Challenge

### Spend the $2,000

You receive **$2,000 that must be spent, not saved**. Create a plan using the money on possessions, experiences, or both. Your partner must challenge at least two choices. Agree on a final plan and explain why it offers the best long-term value.
