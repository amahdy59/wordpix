## 2. Read & Understand

### Freedom or Company?

Having your own home can sound perfect. You choose when to eat, when to sleep, how clean the room should be, and what to watch on television. There is no need to explain your choices to anyone else. For many people, living alone means independence and privacy.

But independence has a price. One person must pay the rent, electricity, internet, and other household costs. If something goes wrong, there may be no one at home to help. Some people also feel lonely when they spend too much time by themselves.

Living with other people can solve some of these problems. Roommates or family members can split the bills, share housework, and provide company. A difficult day may feel easier when someone is there to talk to. On the other hand, sharing a home requires compromise. People may have different ideas about noise, visitors, cleaning, food, or personal space.

Research does not show that simply living alone automatically makes someone unhappy. A systematic review found some evidence linking living alone with lower positive mental health, but the authors also stressed that the available evidence was limited and varied. The important point is that “living alone” and “being lonely” are not exactly the same thing.

Some people love having their own space and still have active social lives. Others live in busy homes but still feel alone.

So perhaps the best living arrangement depends less on the number of people in a home and more on personality, money, relationships, and the kind of life a person wants.

### In Short

Living alone can offer **independence**, **privacy**, and your own space, while sharing a home can provide company and help people **split the bills**. The best choice often depends on whether people can **get along with** one another and make a **compromise** when their habits are different.

## 4. Conversation Toolkit

### Comparing Two Choices

**One advantage of... is...**

> One advantage of living alone is privacy.

**Compared with...**

> Compared with living alone, sharing a home can be cheaper.

**On the other hand...**

> Living with friends can be fun. On the other hand, you may have less privacy.

**For me, ... is more important than...**

> For me, independence is more important than saving money.

## 6. Discussion

### 1. Your Preference

Would you prefer to live alone, with family, with a partner, or with friends? Why?

### 2. Money

Would you give up some privacy to save a large amount of money on rent?

### 3. Habits

Which household habit would be hardest for you to compromise on?

### 4. Roommates

What qualities make someone a good person to live with?

### 5. Different Stages

Do you think the best living arrangement changes with age? How?

### 6. The Big Question

Is independence more valuable than having people around you every day?

## 7. Speaking Challenge

### Choose the Best Home

A friend has three options: **a small apartment alone**, **a larger apartment with two roommates**, or **living with family while saving money**. Discuss the advantages and disadvantages of each option, ask questions about your friend’s priorities, and agree on one recommendation.

### Useful Language

> One advantage of this option is...

> Compared with the other option...

> It depends on what matters most to them.

> I think this would suit them because...
