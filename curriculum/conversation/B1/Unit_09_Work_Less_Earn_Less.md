## 1. Big Question & Warm-up

## 2. Read & Understand

### More Money or More Time?

Imagine your employer offers you a choice. You can continue working five days a week at your current salary, or work four days and earn 15% less. Which option would you take?

Money clearly matters. People need income for housing, food, transport, healthcare, education, and savings. Someone who is already struggling to make ends meet may not have the freedom to exchange money for time.

However, time also has value. Long working hours can reduce time for sleep, exercise, family, friends, hobbies, and rest. The International Labour Organization has reported that more than one-third of workers globally regularly work more than 48 hours per week, and its research connects unwanted long hours with poorer work-life balance.

Some organisations have experimented with shorter working weeks without cutting salary, often trying to maintain productivity by reducing unnecessary meetings or improving work processes. Results from such trials have attracted attention, but one company’s success does not prove the same model will work in every industry.

There is also an important difference between choosing fewer hours and being unable to find enough paid work. A person working part time by choice may enjoy the extra time. A person who needs more hours to pay rent may experience the same schedule very differently.

The question therefore involves a trade-off. Higher income can create security and opportunity. More free time can improve quality of life. The right answer depends on how much money a person needs, how they value time, and what responsibilities they have.

### In Short

Working fewer hours may improve **work-life balance** and **quality of life**, but lower **income** can create financial pressure. Someone who is already trying to **make ends meet** may not be able to **cut down on** work, even if they strongly value more free time.

## 4. Conversation Toolkit

### Talking About Priorities and Trade-offs

**For me, ... is a bigger priority than...**

> For me, free time is a bigger priority than a higher salary.

**The trade-off is...**

> The trade-off is less money for more personal time.

**I would accept... if...**

> I would accept lower pay if my basic costs were covered.

**It would depend on...**

> It would depend on how large the pay cut was.

## 6. Discussion

### 1. Your Choice

Would you take a 15% pay cut for one extra free day each week?

### 2. Priorities

At what stage of life might people value money more than time?

### 3. Employers

Should companies try shorter weeks without reducing salary?

### 4. Productivity

Can people sometimes do the same amount of work in fewer hours? How?

### 5. Fairness

Should highly paid employees and low-paid employees think about this choice differently?

### 6. The Big Question

Which gives more freedom: enough money or enough time?
