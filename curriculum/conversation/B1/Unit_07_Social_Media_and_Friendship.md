## 2. Read & Understand

### More Connected, But Are We Closer?

A friend can now be thousands of kilometres away and still feel present in daily life. People share photos, send voice messages, celebrate birthdays online, and reach out when someone needs support. Social media makes it easier to stay in touch even when work, travel, or family life make face-to-face meetings difficult.

Recent Pew Research Center findings show that many U.S. teenagers see friendship as one area where social media can help more than hurt. In its 2025 survey, 30% said social media helped their friendships, while 7% said it hurt them; many others described the effect as neutral.

But frequent contact is not always the same as a meaningful relationship. A person may keep up with hundreds of people online and still have only a few close friends. Social media can also create comparison. Seeing other people’s parties, holidays, or friendships can make someone feel left out.

Communication online also loses some information. A short message can sound angry even when the writer did not mean it that way. Jokes can be misunderstood because we cannot hear tone of voice or see facial expressions.

On the positive side, online communication can help friendships survive distance. People who might slowly drift apart can continue sharing ordinary moments. Someone going through a difficult time can reach out quickly rather than wait for the next meeting.

Social media therefore does not automatically create strong or weak friendships. It is a tool. A friendship may become stronger when people use that tool to communicate honestly and support one another—but weaker when online contact replaces deeper conversation.

### In Short

Social media can help people **stay in touch**, **reach out** for **support**, and **keep up with** friends who live far away. But online **comparison** and misunderstanding can damage relationships, and people can still **drift apart** even when they follow each other online.

## 4. Conversation Toolkit

### Agreeing and Disagreeing Politely

**I agree because...**

> I agree because social media makes distance easier.

**I see your point, but...**

> I see your point, but online contact can feel shallow.

**I’m not sure I agree.**

> I’m not sure I agree. Some online friendships are very meaningful.

**That’s true in some cases.**

> That’s true in some cases, but it depends on the friendship.

## 6. Discussion

### 1. Connection

Can online-only friendships be as strong as face-to-face friendships?

### 2. Quantity

Is it better to have many friends or a few close friends?

### 3. Comparison

Why do people compare their lives online even when they know posts are selective?

### 4. Conflict

Is it better to solve an argument by text or face to face?

### 5. Distance

Has technology made long-distance friendship easier or less meaningful?

### 6. The Big Question

Does social media strengthen relationships, or simply make communication more frequent?
