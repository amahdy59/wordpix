## 2. Read & Understand

### One Month Without Your Smartphone?

Imagine waking up tomorrow and deciding not to use your smartphone for one month. No social media, maps, mobile banking, music apps, or quick internet searches. You could still use a computer and perhaps a basic phone for calls and texts.

Would this make life better—or simply more difficult?

Smartphones put many useful tools in one place. We use them to stay in touch with friends, check directions, take photos, pay bills, study, work, and solve small problems quickly. For some people, a smartphone is essential because it is their main way to get online.

However, many people also feel that their phones have become a distraction. In a 2026 U.S. survey, 53% of adults said they spent too much time on their smartphones. Forty-five percent said they had tried to reduce their smartphone use during the previous year.

There is also evidence that reducing smartphone internet use may have benefits. In a 2025 experiment, researchers asked people to block mobile internet on their smartphones for two weeks. They could still make calls and send texts, and they could use the internet on other devices. The researchers reported improvements in attention and well-being for many participants. People also spent more time socializing in person, exercising, and being outdoors.

But living without a smartphone is not easy for everyone. Work, transport, banking, family communication, and emergency information may depend on it.

Perhaps the real question is not whether smartphones are good or bad. Maybe the better question is: **Do we control our phones, or do our phones control our time?**

### In Short

Smartphones are **convenient** and help us **stay in touch**, but they can also become a **distraction**. Many people want to **cut back on** their **screen time**, although they may still **depend on** their phones for important parts of daily life.

## 4. Conversation Toolkit

### Giving an Opinion and Explaining Why

**I think...**

> I think smartphones make life easier.

**The main reason is...**

> The main reason is that I use my phone for work.

**On the other hand...**

> Smartphones are convenient. On the other hand, they can be a distraction.

**It depends on...**

> It depends on the person's job and lifestyle.

## 5. Quick Quiz

**1. If something is “essential,” it is:**  
A. expensive B. unnecessary C. very necessary D. entertaining

**2. Which sentence means “I want to use social media less”?**  
A. I want to put social media on. B. I want to cut back on social media. C. I want to stay in touch with social media. D. I want to depend on social media.

**3. If someone is “glued to their phone,” they:**  
A. have broken it B. use it very often C. cannot find it D. need a new one

**4. Complete: “I use video calls to ________ with friends abroad.”**  
A. stay in touch B. cut back C. put away D. depend

**5. What percentage of adults in the cited U.S. survey said they spent too much time on their smartphones?**  
A. 25% B. 45% C. 53% D. 75%

**6. What did participants in the 2025 experiment block?**  
A. All technology B. Phone calls C. Mobile internet on smartphones D. Computers

**7. What did people spend more time doing during the experiment?**  
A. Watching TV B. Shopping online C. Working D. Socializing, exercising, and being outdoors

**8. Why might completely giving up a smartphone be difficult?**  
A. Phones are always expensive. B. Daily tasks may depend on them. C. Basic phones cannot make calls. D. Everyone needs social media.

**9. What is the main idea?**  
A. Everyone should stop using smartphones. B. Smartphones are completely harmful. C. Smartphones are useful, but we should think about how much control they have over our time. D. People should only use basic phones.

**10. Which is correct?**  
A. cut back my screen time B. cut back on my screen time C. cut my screen time back on D. cut on back my screen time

## 6. Discussion

### 1. Your Life

Which smartphone feature would be hardest for you to live without? Why?

### 2. Your Habits

When is your phone useful, and when does it become a **distraction**?

### 3. Make a Choice

You can keep only three functions: calls, messaging, maps, camera, banking, music, email, social media, search, or video. Which three?

### 4. Relationships

If smartphones disappeared tomorrow, would relationships become better or worse?

### 5. Challenge the Idea

Someone says, “People do not need smartphones. They are just too dependent on them.” What might this opinion ignore?

### 6. The Big Question

Which is the bigger problem: people spending too much time on phones, or modern life requiring too much phone use?

## 7. Speaking Challenge

### The 30-Day Smartphone Challenge

You and a partner want to use your smartphones much less for 30 days. Agree on **three functions you can keep**, **when phone use is allowed**, **one activity you must stop completely**, and **two offline activities to replace it**. Finish by creating five rules for your challenge.

### Useful Language

> I think we should keep...

> The main reason is...

> I could live without...

> I’m not sure I agree because...
