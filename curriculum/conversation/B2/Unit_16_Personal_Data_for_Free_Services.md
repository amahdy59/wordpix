## 2. Read & Understand

### If You Are Not Paying With Money, What Are You Paying With?

Search engines, social networks, email services, maps, video platforms, and many mobile apps can be used without paying a direct subscription fee. Their business models may instead depend partly on advertising, data analysis, or other commercial uses of user information.

Personal data can make a service more useful. A map remembers favourite places, a music app learns what you enjoy, and advertising may become more relevant. Many users accept some tracking because the service is convenient and free at the point of use.

But meaningful consent is difficult when data collection is complex. People often click “agree” without reading long privacy policies. In a 2023 Pew survey, 56% of U.S. adults said they often or almost always agreed to privacy policies without reading them, and 81% said they were concerned about how companies use collected data.

The issue goes beyond advertising. The U.S. Federal Trade Commission has investigated “surveillance pricing,” where detailed information such as location, browsing behaviour, or shopping history may be used by intermediaries to help tailor prices or promotions. This shows why people may care not only about what information is collected but how it is later used.

Supporters of data-funded services argue that advertising allows useful tools to remain accessible to people who cannot or do not want to pay subscriptions. Critics answer that “free” should not mean unlimited permission to collect, combine, and sell information.

Power matters in this exchange. A user may technically agree to data collection but still have little ability to negotiate the terms. This is especially important when one service dominates a market or when leaving it means losing access to a community, workplace network, or useful digital history.

A fair exchange would therefore require more than a long policy and an “accept” button. Users would need understandable choices, limits on unexpected uses, and a realistic way to opt out without losing every useful feature.

### In Short

“Free” digital services often involve **personal data**, **tracking**, and **targeted advertising**. Users may technically give **consent**, but real choice becomes questionable when people cannot easily **opt out** or do not understand what they are **giving away** **in exchange for** access.

## 4. Conversation Toolkit

### Making a Concession

**I accept that..., but...**

> I accept that advertising can fund free services, but users still need meaningful privacy choices.

**That benefit comes at the cost of...**

> That benefit comes at the cost of additional data collection.

**I would be willing to... provided that...**

> I would be willing to share location data provided that it is deleted afterward.

**The trade-off becomes unacceptable when...**

> The trade-off becomes unacceptable when data is used for purposes users could not reasonably expect.
