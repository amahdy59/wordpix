## Airport phrases

The authored Airport phrase set includes train-specific phrases. For air travel,
replace `confirm the platform` with **check the gate number**, and replace
`Which platform does it leave from?` with **Which gate does the flight leave from?**
Suggested Arabic: `يتأكد من رقم بوابة الصعود` and
`من أي بوابة تغادر الرحلة؟`. Use **boarding gate** where useful for clarity.

Cambridge distinguishes the railway sense of
[platform](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/platform)
from the airport sense of [gate](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/gate).
The suggested collocations are editorial recommendations; the word entries do
not establish a frequency ranking for these complete phrases.

Suggested examples:

- "Check the gate number on your boarding pass. It may change before departure."
- "Excuse me, which gate does the flight leave from?"
- "After landing, collect your luggage at baggage claim."

The current example "Passengers collected their luggage after the arrival
notice" is grammatical but vague. The replacement gives a usable airport
situation and a natural time relationship.

The Airport `check in` record is labelled B2. Cambridge labels the relevant
[flight sense B1](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/check-in).
Review it as a core B1 phrase rather than labelling the phrase itself advanced.
An optional harder activity can still use a B1 phrase. Teach a concrete pattern:
`check in for a flight`, `check in online`, and `check a bag in`. Distinguish the
verb **check in** from the noun/adjective **check-in**.

Before implementation, update the English/Arabic meanings, examples, checks,
practice, evidence and review metadata together. Verify actual dictionary links;
do not construct a guessed dictionary URL from a whole phrase.

## Answerable scene questions

Several Airport and 3D Printer Lab scenes say only that an adult "compares"
three items, then ask which item is central. The text gives no discriminating
clue. A list of choices is not a practical situation.

Suggested Airport opening scene:

> At the airport, Omar joins a short queue beside a baggage scale. He gives his
> passport to the airline agent, leaves his suitcase on the belt and receives
> a boarding pass. His visa is ready for inspection if required.

Question: **Where does Omar receive his boarding pass and leave his suitcase?**
Expected answer: **check-in counter**. The text now supports the answer.

Suggested clothing scene:

> Omar chooses work clothes from a shop display. A soft, long-sleeved blouse hangs
> beside a cotton T-shirt and a button-down shirt. He compares the fabric and
> neckline before choosing a gift.

Question: **Which garment has soft fabric and the draping neckline described?**
Use distractors that remain distinguishable from the described garment. Verify
the actual image's neckline before adopting this question. A question about
"which target he notices first" mainly tests order recall and gives little
practice with the word's meaning.

Suggested printer scene:

> On the printer, the print head moves along a rail above the build plate.
> A filament spool holds the material, and the extruder feeds it towards the
> nozzle. The technician identifies each part before discussing the print.

Question: **Which assembly moves above the build plate during printing?**
Expected answer: **Print Head**. Keep this a language example rather than a
technical operating procedure.

## Connected articles and assessment

The 3D Printer Lab reading repeats "the choice depends on the role of each term"
after vocabulary lists. It also starts "At the the 3d printer lab". Replace the
template with a connected article about a concrete task. Explain relationships
between parts and include enough details for gist, detail and inference checks.
An inference question must have textual evidence and a meaningful expected
answer; "the task or situation described" cannot identify a misconception.

Use a short article with a clear opening, two linked events and a conclusion.
Vary verbs naturally, introduce vocabulary through actions and avoid forcing
15 words into every production response. A short response using two or three
target words gives a useful first practice; a later extension can combine more.
