# Usage sources and evidence policy

Research date: 2026-09-29. This policy records resources inspected for their purpose. It does not claim that all existing WordPix examples have been checked against them.

## Authoring sequence

Use [Paul Nation's four strands](https://www.wgtn.ac.nz/lals/resources/paul-nations-resources/paul-nations-publications/publications/documents/2007-Four-strands.pdf) as a course-level design reference: balance meaning-focused input, meaning-focused output, deliberate language study, and fluency practice. This is a balance across the course, not a requirement to divide each usage card into four equal parts. Cambridge's [research on retrieval schedules for collocations](https://www.cambridge.org/core/product/identifier/S0272263124000184/type/journal_article) also warrants caution about universal spacing claims: outcomes vary by task and knowledge measure. Provide repeated retrieval and delayed checks; evaluate retention rather than promising a single optimal schedule.

1. Define the learner's purpose and the exact sense: for example, asking to reschedule an appointment rather than mentioning an appointment.
2. Select a useful phrase or collocation. Check learner dictionaries and, when available, a relevant corpus. Record evidence honestly; leave inaccessible checks pending.
3. Write an original short example with enough context to infer meaning. Use unfamiliar vocabulary sparingly and gloss it when needed.
4. Ask a specific comprehension question with one defensible answer. Include plausible distractors and explanatory feedback.
5. Ask the learner to retrieve the phrase, then use it in a different personally relevant situation. Revisit it in later lessons with varied contexts and feedback.
6. Review the scene, exercise, reading, and image brief together. An image may support comprehension; it must not be required to infer an invisible anatomical structure or an abstract phrase.

## Optional higher levels

Keep the core lesson independently completable. An optional “Explore more” interaction should contain separately authored and reviewed extensions: a new sense, collocation, register contrast, or communicative task. Do not relabel the existing reading one CEFR level higher based on arithmetic.

For example, an original progression could introduce “change the date” in a simple request, then “reschedule an appointment,” and later “postpone the launch pending a safety review.” These are illustrative teaching sequences, not verified CEFR assignments.

For B2–C1, use news alongside workplace discussions, interviews, culture, science, and everyday disagreement. Teach transferable expressions, such as evaluating evidence and explaining consequences. Headline compression and journalistic idioms need explicit register labels. High-level work should require interpretation and precise expression, not merely longer sentences.

## Evidence recorded per authored item

- Stable lesson/scene ID and revision.
- Target word or phrase, exact sense, communicative purpose, and register.
- Intended CEFR, whether estimated or entry-verified, and reference URL.
- Frequency/collocation source, actual query or entry, date checked, and result or pending status.
- Original/authored, quotation, or adaptation status; source article URL/date for real-world references and applicable reuse permission.
- Scenario, comprehension question, answer, explanation, production task, and optional extension.
- Editorial review status, image brief revision, and image approval status.

Use original WordPix sentences by default. Short attributed quotations may be appropriate after checking reuse conditions; do not bulk copy dictionary or news example banks. News-inspired invented situations must be identifiable as fictional, and real-event claims must be traceable to a dated source.

## Coverage and completion

Define a bounded inventory of priority senses and phrases. Measure coverage against that inventory; do not promise “all usages below B2.” Track each field's review status separately. Corpus coverage or the presence of a target in an existing passage does not establish that the passage is suitable for a particular lesson.

The current generic-template detector is a triage aid only. It can flag legitimate “deal with” examples and miss other empty templates. Every scene still needs an editorial check before image production.
