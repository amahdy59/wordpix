VISION
Vision Statement
An AI-native English-learning app that fuses the immersion and pronunciation rigor of Rosetta Stone with the habit-forming gamification of Duolingo, then goes further with AI-driven, multisensory experiences — short generated stories, adaptive conversation practice, and phoneme-level pronunciation coaching — that make vocabulary genuinely memorable rather than merely recognizable.
CORE PRINCIPLE
Words Are Never Learned in Isolation
Every word enters the app already embedded in a scene, gets drilled individually, then gets recombined into a sentence, a short reading passage, and a short video — so "mastery" means being able to use a word in a new context, not just recall its translation.
IN ONE SENTENCE
Product Summary
Learners acquire English in small, scene-based vocabulary clusters (10–20 words), drill each word through escalating recognition → recall → pronunciation → production exercises, then immediately reuse that vocabulary in a sentence, a reading passage, and a short AI-generated video — with mastered words resurfacing on a spaced-repetition schedule so retention, not just first-pass completion, is what the app actually optimizes for.

Competitive Research
Analysis of Duolingo, Rosetta Stone, and spaced-repetition tools — what to keep, what to fix, and where the market gap lies.
DUOLINGO
Duolingo — Keep vs. Fix
✓ Keep: Bite-sized Lessons
✓ Keep: Streaks & Rewards
✓ Keep: AI Personalization
3–5 min lessons that most users can complete without friction. A core reason the app doesn't feel like a chore.
Over 3 million users with 365+ day streaks. Loss-aversion is extremely effective at building daily habit.
AI-assisted adaptive difficulty, free grammar explanations, and AI roleplay/conversation features in premium tier.
✗ Fix: Pattern Matching
✗ Fix: Thin Speaking Practice
✗ Fix: Content Quality

Context feels artificial. Speaking practice is thin or premium-gated. Exercises repeat in structure even as vocabulary changes.
AI-first content pivot led to user complaints about repetitive and less culturally nuanced content. Best features sit behind paywall.
ROSETTA STONE
Rosetta Stone — Keep vs. Fix
✓ Keep: Dynamic Immersion
✓ Keep: TruAccent
✓ Keep: Structured Curriculum
No translation crutch. Connecting image/sound directly to meaning forces the learner to think rather than just react.
Real-time pronunciation correction — listens as the learner repeats and prompts correction until pronunciation is right.
Core lesson split into mini-sessions, followed by supplemental drills. Moving toward AI conversation practice (Chat Missions) in 2026.
✗ Fix: No Grammar Explanation
✗ Fix: Fixed Content
✗ Fix: Motivation & Price
Frustrates learners who want to understand why a language works a certain way. Makes early progress feel slow.
A locked set of stock photos and scripted phrases rather than adaptive, personalized material. No real conversational practice.
Less motivating design, slower perceived progression, limited social/personalization layer, premium pricing barrier.
MARKET OPPORTUNITY
The Gap — What No One Does
Every category solves one piece and neglects the others:

• Habit-and-breadth apps (Duolingo) → shallow context, thin speaking practice
• Immersion apps (Rosetta Stone) → deep context and pronunciation, but weak motivation and no personalization
• SRS tools (Anki) → strong retention, but no immersion, story, or conversation

The opportunity: treat vocabulary not as a list to memorize but as a lived experience — across scenes, stories, conversations, and spaced reuse over time. This is the space none of the three categories occupies alone.

Learning Science Foundations
Established second-language-acquisition (SLA) principles the product design is built on top of.
FOUNDATIONS
Six Core Principles
Comprehensible Input
Spaced Repetition
Active Recall
Krashen: Lessons deliver content just above the learner's current level — mostly known words plus 1–2 new concepts per exposure, never an overload.
Words resurface on increasing intervals (roughly 1, 3, 7, 14, 30 days), dynamically adjusted by performance — success rate, response speed, and confidence.
Learners must remember, pronounce, spell, and use a word from memory — not just recognize it. Actively producing a word cements it far better than re-reading.
Contextual Learning
Dual Coding / Multisensory
Desirable Difficulty
Words introduced through pictures, situations, stories, conversations, and video rather than translation lists. Grammar inferred from examples with optional explanations.
Every vocabulary item links image, audio, text, interaction, and video — the more senses involved, the stronger and more durable the memory trace.
Exercises escalate in difficulty within a cluster (recognize → recall → produce → combine). Older vocabulary keeps resurfacing alongside new clusters.

Core Learning Loop
The heart of the product — Cluster → Drill → Combine → Immerse → Retain. Defines what a "session" and a "word" actually mean.
MACRO LOOP
1. Cluster (Learn)
1. Cluster (Learn)
Introduce 10–20 new words tied to one real-life scene.

• Image + native audio + phonetic breakdown per word
• No default translation (tap-to-reveal safety net)
• Multiple-choice recognition ("tap 'door'")
• Audio-only comprehension check
2. Drill (Use)
2. Drill (Use)
Build individual word fluency.

• Pronunciation with phoneme-level AI feedback
• Spelling practice (letter-tile assembly)
• Production from audio alone (no text shown)
3. Combine (Use)
3. Combine (Use)
Genuine sentence-level production — the piece neither competitor does well.

• Sentence-tile assembly ("The / door / is / red")
• Free-form original sentences mixing new + mastered vocabulary
• Auto-checked for grammar
4. Immerse (Read/Watch/Speak)
4. Immerse (Read/Watch/Speak)
Reuse the whole cluster in one coherent scene.

• 3–5 sentence reading passage (cluster + prior mastered words)
• 15–30s AI-generated short video of the scene
• Spoken description task ("What color is the door?")
• AI conversation roleplay using only learned vocabulary
5. Retain (Review → Master)
5. Retain (Review → Master)
Prove mastery through delayed reuse, not one-time quiz.

• Mastery lifecycle: New → Recognized → Practiced → Confident → Mastered
• Words resurface in later sessions on spaced schedule
• Words can decay back a stage if not reviewed in time
WORKED EXAMPLE
One Cluster Walkthrough
Cluster: red, blue, green, door, chair, table, window, room, open, close

1. See an image of a door, hear "The door," repeat it aloud.
2. Recognize it among distractor images when the audio plays.
3. Pronounce it — AI flags the specific sound that was off, not just right/wrong.
4. Spell it by arranging letter tiles.
5. Build a sentence: "The door is red."
6. Read a short passage: "The room has a red door. The window is open."
7. Watch a 30–60s AI-generated scene using the same lines, spoken naturally.
8. Answer questions about the scene: "What color is the door?"
9. Describe the scene aloud in an original sentence; get light pronunciation/grammar feedback.
10. Have a short roleplay conversation limited to today's (and prior mastered) vocabulary.
11. The cluster's words enter the spaced-repetition queue for the Retain stage.
CURRICULUM PROGRESSION
How Clusters Chain Together
Words → Phrases → Sentences → Paragraphs → Stories → Conversations → Real-life situations

Each layer reuses and extends the vocabulary that came before it, so knowledge compounds rather than resetting every session.

AI's Role — Coach, Not Content Generator
AI functions as a personal language coach across five concrete jobs, not as an undifferentiated 'AI-powered' label.
FIVE AI JOBS
How AI Serves the Learner
1. Adaptive Lesson Generation
2. Pronunciation Coach
Detects specific words/skills a learner struggles with. Generates targeted extra practice. Adjusts session length and difficulty to performance and engagement.
Phoneme-level analysis against native-speaker audio. Visually highlights the specific mispronounced sound with a targeted tip (e.g., "your 'th' sounds like 't' — place your tongue between your teeth").
3. Story/Scene Generator
4. Conversation Partner
Produces short scenes/stories using only known vocabulary plus 1–2 new words. Tunable genres (daily life, travel, workplace, light adventure) matched to the learner's level.
Simulates realistic situations (ordering food, airport check-in, job interview). Adapts speech speed, vocabulary, and follow-up complexity to the learner's current level.
5. Feedback Engine
Explains why an answer is right or wrong and what alternative phrasings are acceptable, rather than issuing a bare correct/incorrect verdict.

Gamification & Motivation Model
Rewards mastery and consistency, not just speed or volume — a deliberate correction to XP/streak-only models.
MECHANICS
Core Gamification Elements
Per-Word Mastery Lifecycle
No Hard Fail-State
Vocabulary World Map
New → Recognized → Practiced → Confident → Mastered. Visible to the learner (not a hidden algorithm) so they can see exactly why a word isn't "done" yet. Words can decay a stage if left unreviewed.
Replace "hearts"/lives with a soft consequence — a mistake shortens that word's next spaced-repetition interval instead of costing a life. Keeps consequence without punishment.
Themed worlds (Home, School, Food, Nature, Travel, Business, Health) — each a collection of scene-based clusters, giving the app a visual "world" to explore.
Achievements
Daily Missions (Varied)
Retention-Scored Leaderboards
100 words mastered, a perfect pronunciation streak, 7-day review consistency, first full conversation completed, 50 stories read.
10 pronunciation reps, one full story, a review-the-forgotten-words session, or one conversation scenario — rotating so the daily habit doesn't feel identical.
Ranked by words still retained after 30 days, not raw XP — discourages rushing through content just to climb a leaderboard.

Signature Features & Differentiators
The concepts that differentiate this app from every existing English learning tool on the market.
DIFFERENTIATING CONCEPTS
Signature Features
Scene-Based Clusters
Memory Palace
Every 10–20 word set is grounded in one real scene (a kitchen, an airport, a job interview). This makes reading passages and videos feel coherent, not like disconnected flashcards.
A revisitable virtual environment where objects represent mastered vocabulary. Uses spatial memory as an additional retrieval aid and a visual "home" for everything mastered.
Vocabulary Webs
Mistake Journal
A visual map of semantic relationships (red → colors → clothing → shopping). Learners build a network of connected words instead of a flat list.
Automatically logs recurring errors (mispronounced sounds, grammar slips) and turns them into a personalized, self-updating practice set.
Cultural Capsules
Multiple Listening Modes
Short optional cards on idioms, tone, and everyday cultural context tied to the current scene. A direct answer to the "AI content feels generic" criticism.
Slow speech, natural speed, and multiple accents (American, British, Australian) unlocked as the learner advances.
Real-Life Challenges
Sentence Gallery
Weekly tasks like reading a restaurant menu, understanding a weather forecast, or recording a short self-introduction — vocabulary applied to real encounters.
A learner's own "Combine"-stage sentences compiled over time into a personal, growing storybook of what they've learned to say.
Bite-size / Deep-dive Toggle
Progress Analytics
A 3–5 minute "just the streak" mode alongside a 15–20 minute full-cluster mode, so the app doesn't force one session length on everyone.
Dashboard covering vocabulary size, pronunciation accuracy, reading comprehension, speaking activity, and review consistency — not just a point total.
